# Rock 3: Content Services Proposal — Feedback & Response

**May 31, 2026**

---

## Jordan's Question 1

> "I think it would be helpful to have a breakdown of what we are currently doing with the technical SEO package. Then a breakdown of what we think the technical SEO package needs to look like and what the content package needs to look like in order for us to maximize results as we enter the AI search update."

**Snell's response:**

That's fair, and easily addressed. Our current SEO offer covers on-site work: meta titles, meta descriptions, and occasional H1 changes and optimizations. While we do add some content pieces, during the process of structuring H2s, H3s, and the HTML hierarchy, we are not changing the text of those specific headings.

The content proposal addresses two major changes in the AI era:

1. **Query fan-out:** We enhance content and add as much as possible across all URLs to rank for the sub-queries that AI generates when users ask it to search the internet.
2. **Paradigm shift:** Traditional SEO asks, "Can this page rank for this query?" Modern AI search asks, "Can this source help the AI satisfy the user's intent or complete the user's task?" Google's I/O 2026 announcement ("A New Era for AI Search") suggests new search agents will be able to perform tasks directly within the search engine. We need to distinguish: is a human searching (satisfying intent) or is an agent searching (completing a task)? This matters because sometimes an agent will try to purchase an item, and other times it will be an actual user.

## Jordan's Question 2

> "You talk about how with the content package we will include a service where we do small updates to several pages per month such as product descriptions, headline updates, etc. That is currently already part of the technical SEO package. To be honest, I think Nick might be the only one taking advantage of that."

**Snell's response:**

That is not entirely accurate. The current technical SEO package focuses on technical tasks and very small content-related tasks. We are not actually creating or adding new tags to all URLs on a website. We fix HTML, we perform on-sites, but we are not performing real content optimizations.

When I talk about creating content, it is not just writing for the sake of it. It needs to be strategic: built as clusters to satisfy transactional search intent.

**Supporting data:** From multiple industry sources, more than 90% of informational queries now show Google's AI overview box. However, for transactional queries — hiring services, purchasing an item, looking for a business — Google still shows the traditional search results page. Traditional SEO remains valuable for transactional keywords: making a call, filling out a form, completing a purchase.

---

## Jordan's Question 3

> "Maybe your recommendation is that we stop selling the technical SEO package with the content piece and focus instead on certain merchant center updates."

**Snell's response:**

The current technical SEO package is not covering all the content we can take control over. My proposal is to take ownership of content creation so we can enhance the brand's online presence and put the focus on transactional keywords.

---

## Summary

1. The content proposal complements, not duplicates, the technical SEO package. Real content optimization is simply not being done today.
2. The AI search era demands two new things: query fan-out and answering to AI agents, not just human users.
3. Transactional queries remain traditional SEO territory — that is where we focus our content efforts.
4. Owning content creation means controlling the keywords that convert.
